Briefing · The Context Graph
Decision traces · Institutional memory · Human-in-the-loop

Our systems record what happened.
iPDLC records why it was allowed to happen.

Every product that ships at Citi is the sum of hundreds of judgment calls — approvals, exceptions, trade-offs made between a person and, now, an agent. Today that judgment evaporates the moment it's exercised. The context graph is the layer that keeps it: a permanent, replayable record of each decision, captured at the moment it is made, that turns this quarter's exceptions into next quarter's precedent.

The problem

Where product-development decisions die today

The product development lifecycle runs on decisions that no system of record stores. Tap each one — this is not a data-quality problem; the reasoning was never treated as data in the first place.

Enterprise systems of record — the CRMs, the trackers, the ledgers — store outcomes. None of them was built to store judgment.

Why it compounds

Every run makes the next one better-informed

Decisions link to the durable things they touch — regulations, systems, capabilities. Shared touchpoints connect new work to old judgment, even when the ideas are worded nothing alike. Advance the quarters and watch precedent accumulate around the same few entities.

What it earns

Precedent buys back senior attention — deliberately

The graph is not an archive; it is the evidence base for reducing review burden on repeats, one narrow class of decision at a time. Tap each stage.

Guardrail, stated plainly: nothing enters institutional memory without a named human decision attached, and every remembered claim traces back to that person and their words. That sentence is the model-risk review, condensed.
Why this is ours to build

The capture has to happen where the decision happens

a

iPDLC is the execution path. Every approval, exception, and hand-off in the lifecycle already commits through a transaction the platform controls — so the decision and its full context are captured in the same instant, never reconstructed afterwards from fragments.

b

Vendor platforms record state, not judgment. A tracker knows the permit was granted; it cannot know it was an exception, who had the authority, or what the approver saw. Bolting capture on after the fact recovers none of that.

c

The asset compounds in-house. Built inside Citi, the graph of how Citi decides — its exceptions, its precedents, its named judgment — accrues to Citi, on Citi's entitlements, inside Citi's audit perimeter. That is the difference between buying automation and building institutional memory.
